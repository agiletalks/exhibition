Define / Problem Framing

Making Sense of Chaos: Stakeholder Themes, Insights, and HMW Translation

Faced with complex social and industrial conditions, the easiest mistake to make when applying design thinking is to rush into solutions based on scattered interview quotes. In the Discover stage, we conducted dense coding on 19 interviews—our "factual divergence phase". In the Define stage, the team's job is convergence: clustering hundreds of codes from different interviewees using an Affinity Diagram, extracting Themes and Insights, and then through deep discussion and prioritization, translating the most high-leverage problems into HMW (How Might We) challenges. Below is our convergence trajectory.

II. Affinity Diagram Convergence: Clustering Codes into Themes

A single stakeholder's words represent only a partial perspective. For example, while producers complain about cost, migrants worry about trust, and experiencers are confused by tedious farming tasks. If we only look at one side, the solution will be un-balanced. Our Affinity Diagram work was to lay out code cards from all 19 interviews, finding common patterns and tensions across roles (such as producers and pioneers, migrants and promoters). Through comparison, the teams clustered hundreds of codes into key themes.

III. Prioritization & Selection: Mapping HMW Challenges

Faced with dozens of insights, the groups used a digital HMW board to classify challenges into "overcoming barriers", "creating positive outcomes", and "leveraging strengths". Through voting and card sorting, we translated the most high-leverage pain points into HMW design challenges.

Team A Selection Path

Smallholder Branding & Value Chain Transformation

Key Theme: #Technical Compromise & Market Differentiation
Team Discussion & Prioritization

Team A debated "technical compromise" and "financial risks." Reviewing Ah-Cong's and Lai Qing-song's work, they realized blind spending on complex processors causes heavy family stress, whereas liquid molasses serves as a low-cost, differentiating "technical compromise." The team voted to focus on a branding toolkit rather than buying heavy equipment.

Selected Core Insight

Organic smallholders face technical bottlenecks (like lime-free sugar processing) that prevent solid block sugar production. Blind capital spending causes severe financial/family risks. Liquid molasses serves as a low-cost compromise, but needs alignment with cooking habits for differentiation. Smallholders need a branding toolkit to validate MVP value and map resources.

HMW Challenge Translation

How might we design a low-barrier branding toolkit for smallholders to catalog crop resources, map customers, and validate MVP values?

Team B Selection Path

Booking Flow & Relationship Continuity

Key Theme: #Long-term Internships & Homestay Immersion
Team Discussion & Prioritization

Team B faced 10 themes on experience design. They debated host capacity limits and relationship disconnection after visits. The team combined booking flow pain points with homestay immersion. Single-visit marketing waste and low retention were verified as key limiters, voting to focus on relationship continuity.

Selected Core Insight

Farming experiences face broken journeys: booking is scattered, anticipation fades before arrival, and no follow-up follows visits. This yields high acquisition costs for one-time tourists rather than building long-term community support for ecological farming.

HMW Challenge Translation

How might we integrate scattered booking channels and design level-based journeys to sustain traveler anticipation and connection?

Team C Selection Path

Childcare Support & Co-learning Networks

Key Theme: #Intergenerational Care & Multi-local Family
Team Discussion & Prioritization

Team C focused on rural families. Smallholder parents face severe time conflicts between labor and childcare. After comparing urban nurseries with local neighborhood childcare, the team prioritized mutual childcare platforms as the solution that best leverages local social capital.

Selected Core Insight

Lack of rural childcare resources forces families to rely on complex grandparent arrangements or dual-tracking. Smallholder parents face double time constraints during busy seasons, requiring geographic and value-aligned mutual childcare support.

HMW Challenge Translation

How might we combine geographic locations and educational concepts to build a low-barrier mutual childcare and co-learning platform, easing parenting stress during busy seasons?

Team D Selection Path

Social Integration & Life Guides for Migrants

Key Theme: #Urban-Rural Migration & Georelations Network
Team Discussion & Prioritization

Team D debated georelations, leasing fears, and individual support systems. They realized mainstream rental platforms fail in rural areas; leasing land relies on local connections. The team voted to prioritize reducing this georelations barrier through a guide that makes implicit rules explicit.

Selected Core Insight

When youth move to rural areas, the primary barrier is establishing trust and local networks, not agricultural techniques. Land rules and local social structures lack transparent translation, causing early social friction and failed migration projects.

HMW Challenge Translation

How might we translate land leasing, income strategies, and local social rules into a three-stage integration guide, reducing barriers for new migrants?

Next: Step into the Develop Phase
